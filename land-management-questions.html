Common Land Management Questions

Real questions from Central Texas landowners · Serving Coryell, Bell & Lampasas counties

These are the real questions Central Texas landowners ask us — about mesquite and cedar coming back, whether mulching beats a dozer, what clearing costs, permits, and more. Short, straight answers below. If yours isn't here, call or text 737-235-1335 and we'll tell you straight.

Will mesquite grow back after you mulch it?

Yes — mesquite resprouts thicker from a bud zone 12 to 18 inches underground when you only cut or mulch the top. To actually kill it we grub the root crown out mechanically, or you pair mulching with a follow-up treatment.

Will cedar grow back after clearing?

Cedar (Ashe juniper) comes back from seed in the soil, not from the ground-level stump. So the first clearing is a reset — a realistic plan includes catching the seedlings before they re-establish.

Does forestry mulching kill trees or just cut them?

Mulching grinds growth to the ground and stops most brush cold, but species with aggressive roots like mesquite and cedar can resprout. For a permanent kill on those, the root has to be removed or treated.

How many acres can you clear in a day?

Roughly half an acre to two acres, depending on brush density, tree size, and terrain. Light growth on flat ground goes fast; heavy mature cedar on rocky ground is slower.

Is forestry mulching better than a dozer?

For most overgrown-land jobs, yes — mulching leaves a soil-protecting chip layer instead of bare, compacted dirt, and there's nothing to haul off. A dozer earns its place when you need bare, graded dirt to build on.

Do I have to haul off the mulch?

No. Forestry mulching leaves the ground material in place as a mulch layer that holds moisture, fights erosion, and breaks down into the soil over time.

Can you clear land in the summer, or only winter?

We clear year-round. Every season works — cedar and most brush can be mulched any month — so there's no reason to wait on a season if your land needs clearing now.

Do you spray herbicide?

No — we're a mechanical operation (mulching, clearing, grubbing, stump grinding). Where a chemical follow-up makes sense, that's a separate step handled by you or a licensed applicator, and we'll tell you when a job calls for it.

Will clearing cedar help my water or springs?

It can. A mature Ashe juniper transpires roughly 33 gallons a day, and on the right ground, removing dense cedar has measurably improved soil moisture and spring flow.

Do I need a permit to clear land in Texas?

On most private rural land in Coryell, Bell, and Lampasas counties you generally don't — but floodplains, waterways, endangered-species habitat, city limits, and HOA rules are real exceptions. Always verify locally first.

What does land clearing cost?

Forestry mulching runs roughly $1,500 to $6,000 per acre depending on density and terrain; other services are quoted by the job. Every price is a guide until we see the site.
