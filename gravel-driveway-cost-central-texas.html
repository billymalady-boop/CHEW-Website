Gravel Driveway Cost in Central Texas: Full Breakdown

July 2026 · 7 min read · Serving Coryell, Bell & Lampasas counties

A gravel driveway in Central Texas is priced by length, width, depth of stone, and the base work your ground needs — not a single flat rate. A short, flat drive on solid ground costs far less than a long drive that needs base repair and drainage. Every job is quoted after a site visit, and a minimum job price applies.

What Actually Drives the Cost

Five things set the price of a gravel driveway. Length and width determine the surface area and the tonnage of stone. Depth of stone — a proper drive is built in layers, and more depth means more material. Base prep — whether the ground needs grading, a compacted base, or fabric under the stone. Drainage — crown, culverts, or ditching to keep water from destroying the drive. And access and length of haul for getting material to the site. A flat, short, well-draining drive is straightforward; a long rural drive with soft spots and water problems is a bigger job.

Gravel Types and Why They Matter

A driveway that lasts is built in layers, not dumped as one. A coarse base stone (often crushed concrete or large crusher run) goes down first for structure, topped with a finer surface layer like crushed limestone or screenings that packs tight and drives smooth. Using the wrong stone — or skipping the base — is the single most common reason a cheap driveway turns to washboard and potholes within a year.

New Install vs. Repair

A fresh install prices differently than a repair. Repairing an existing drive — regrading, filling ruts and potholes, adding a fresh top layer, fixing the crown — often costs a fraction of a full rebuild and buys years more life. If your drive still has a decent base, repair is usually the smart money. If it's failed down to mud, it may need rebuilding.

How Much Stone You'll Need

Tonnage is where a lot of quotes go wrong. You can get a solid estimate yourself with our gravel driveway calculator — plug in your length, width, and depth and it returns the tons and cubic yards. That tells you the material side; the labor, grading, base, and drainage are what we quote after seeing the site.

How We Quote and How to Keep It Down

We quote the whole job after a site visit, with a minimum job price, so you know the number before we start. To keep cost reasonable: repair before the base fails rather than rebuilding later, get the drainage right the first time so you're not re-graveling every year, and match the stone to the job.
