Prickly Pear Removal in Central Texas: Don't Mow It, Ever

By Billy Malady, Owner/Operator · July 2026 · 6 min read · Serving Coryell, Bell & Lampasas counties

Never mow prickly pear. Mowing shreds the pads, and every fragment left on the ground can root into a brand-new plant — so one pass turns a patch into a field. Real control means removing the root crown a few inches below the surface, collecting every pad for disposal, and following up with herbicide on any regrowth. It's a management job, not a one-pass fix.

This is one I feel strongly about, because I've seen folks make their prickly pear problem five times worse in a single afternoon. If you've got prickly pear on your Central Texas property and somebody tells you to just mow it down — don't. Here's what actually happens, and what actually works.

Why You Should Never Mow Prickly Pear

Prickly pear spreads from its pads. When a mower hits it, it doesn't kill the plant — it chops it into dozens of pieces and flings them across your ground. Each of those pieces can take root and start a new colony. So mowing doesn't reduce prickly pear; it plants it. You go from a prickly pear problem to a prickly pear disaster in one pass, and you'll be fighting it across a much bigger area the next season.

How Prickly Pear Comes Back

Two things make this plant so stubborn. First, it re-roots from a single stray pad — miss one on the ground and you've left a seed for a new plant. Second, even if you clear off all the pads, it regrows from the root crown, which sits a few inches below the soil surface. Cut it at ground level and leave that crown, and it comes right back. That's why surface-only removal never finishes the job.

What Actually Works

Controlling prickly pear is a two-part effort — physical removal, then follow-up:

  • Get the root crown out. Physical removal means lifting the plant and its crown from about 2 to 4 inches below the surface, not just knocking off the pads.
  • Collect every pad. Any pad that comes loose has to be gathered and hauled off for disposal — never left lying on the ground, where it will re-root.
  • Follow up with herbicide on the regrowth. Even with careful removal, some regrowth is normal, and a targeted herbicide treatment on that regrowth is what makes control stick. Spraying isn't something we do — that's a job for you or a licensed applicator — but it's the step that keeps the removal from being temporary.

Eradication vs. Management — Be Realistic

Here's the honest part: complete eradication of prickly pear is rare. Most landowners end up managing and controlling it rather than wiping it out entirely, and that's a perfectly good outcome — open, usable land you keep in check over time. Anyone who promises you a one-pass, gone-forever fix on prickly pear is setting you up to be frustrated.

How CHEW Helps

Our skid steer with a grapple can move, pile, and gather prickly pear for disposal — the heavy, thorny work of getting the pads off your ground and out of your way. That's the mechanical side, and it's real progress. But I want you going into it with the full picture: this is a management situation, not a one-and-done. We handle the clearing; you pair it with the crown removal and a herbicide follow-up (through a licensed applicator) for lasting control. I'd rather tell you that up front than have you call me frustrated later.

The Bottom Line

Prickly pear is manageable, but it is not a one-and-done situation. The landowners who win against it go in with a realistic plan — physical removal done right, every pad hauled off, and a follow-up treatment — not just one pass with a machine and a hope.
