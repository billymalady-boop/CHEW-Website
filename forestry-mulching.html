Forestry Mulching

One machine, one pass. We grind cedar, brush, and small trees into a mulch layer that protects your soil — no burning, no hauling, no torn-up ground. Serving Coryell, Bell & Lampasas counties.

What Forestry Mulching Is

Forestry mulching is a single-machine clearing method. Our JCB track loader runs a mulching head that cuts, grinds, and spreads vegetation in one pass — trees, brush, and stumps processed on the spot and left as a fine layer of wood-chip mulch across the ground. There's no separate crew to fell, pile, haul, or burn. That's what makes it faster and cleaner than old-style clearing, and why the ground is left ready to use instead of scraped bare.

What It Clears — and the Size Limits

Mulching is the right tool for cedar (Ashe juniper), mesquite, saplings, heavy brush, and small-to-mid-size trees. We handle material efficiently up to the size range a mulcher is built for; genuinely large, mature hardwoods fall outside what the machine takes down, and we'll tell you that straight during the site visit rather than after we start. If a few trees are too big, we'll flag them and talk through options.

Why the Mulch Layer Is a Benefit, Not a Mess

The shredded material that stays on your ground is doing real work. It slows rainfall runoff and protects topsoil while the ground is bare — which matters on Central Texas slopes and after our heavy rain events. It holds moisture in dry stretches, breaks down into organic matter that feeds the soil, and a thick enough layer helps hold back the next wave of cedar seedlings. For pasture reclamation, that mulch can be knocked down thinner so grass seed makes soil contact. (More on that in our guide to slowing cedar regrowth.)

Who It's For

Landowners and ranchers reclaiming overgrown acreage. Homeowners who want their property walkable again. Anyone clearing a homesite, opening pasture, cutting fence lines, reducing wildfire fuel, or getting to the back of land that's been locked behind a cedar wall for years. If the goal is usable ground with the soil left intact, this is usually the method.

Common Questions

What's the difference between mulching and brush cutting?

Brush cutting knocks vegetation down and clears it — faster and more affordable when you just need it cut. Forestry mulching grinds everything into a fine mulch left on-site, for a cleaner finish that also helps slow regrowth. We'll recommend the right one for your goals and how dense the growth is.

Do you haul the material away?

No — the mulch stays on-site by design, working for your soil. If you specifically need material hauled off, tell us and we'll talk through it at the estimate.

Will the cleared brush grow back?

Cedar and brush are aggressive here, so some regrowth is natural. Mulching slows it more than cutting alone, and for ongoing upkeep we offer a scheduled Clear & Keep plan.

Can you work on hilly or rocky ground?

Within reason — our equipment is built for varied Central Texas terrain, but very steep slopes carry safety limits we won't exceed. We'll evaluate your ground at the site visit and tell you straight.
