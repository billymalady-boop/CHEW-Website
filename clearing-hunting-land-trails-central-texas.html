Clearing Hunting Land & Trails in Central Texas

July 2026 · 6 min read · Serving Coryell, Bell & Lampasas counties

Forestry mulching is ideal for hunting land in Central Texas — it cuts shooting lanes, trails, and food-plot openings while leaving a natural mulch layer and your mast-producing trees standing. It improves access and browse without stripping the habitat game depend on. For wildlife, selective clearing beats bulldozing every time.

What Hunters Actually Want From Clearing

Good hunting land is a balance: open enough to move, see, and shoot — but with the cover, browse, and mast that hold game on your property. The goal isn't to clear everything; it's to shape the land. That's exactly what forestry mulching does well, because it's selective. You take out the cedar and junk brush and keep the oaks and cover that matter.

Why Mulching Fits Hunting Land

A mulcher works tree by tree, so you decide what stays. It grinds cedar and brush into a mulch layer that suppresses the next wave of undergrowth and feeds the soil, while leaving your mast trees — the oaks that drop acorns — standing. And clearing the understory actually creates edge habitat and fresh browse at ground level, the transition zones deer and other game favor. You improve the hunting and the habitat in the same pass.

Shooting Lanes and Trails

Clean shooting lanes off a stand or blind, and trails to get in quietly without tearing up the property, are the most common requests. Mulching cuts them precisely, leaves a firm mulched surface to walk or ride, and doesn't leave the stumps, ruts, and bare dirt that dozing does. Trails hold up better and blend into the property instead of scarring it.

Food Plots and Openings

Mulching opens up cedar-choked areas into plantable ground for food plots and clearings that draw and hold game. The chip layer helps hold moisture for what you plant, and because the soil isn't stripped or compacted like it is with a dozer, seed establishes better. Small, well-placed openings often do more for your hunting than clearing wide-open acreage.

Keep the Right Trees, Manage the Cedar

The trees you keep matter as much as what you clear — mast producers, good cover, and travel corridors. The cedar you remove pays off beyond sightlines: it frees up water and browse for the wildlife you're managing for (more on that in our cedar and water guide).
